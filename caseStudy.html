DEEPFAKE FRAUD 2024

Arup Deepfake Fraud

Background

Deepfake technology has developed to the point where video and audio impersonation can appear highly realistic, especially in professional settings. In corporate environments, where employees are used to trusting video meetings and executive instructions, this creates a serious risk.

What Happened?

Employees at engineering firm Arup were reportedly deceived during a fake video conference that appeared to include trusted senior executives. The people on the call looked and sounded genuine, which made the instructions to transfer funds seem believable. As a result, the employees trusted the request and authorised a large transfer that was later found to be manipulated.

Why It Was Convincing?

The attack was effective because it combined realistic visual impersonation, believable voice cloning, and the formal setting of a business meeting. Employees were less likely to question the request because it appeared to come from senior leadership in a familiar professional environment.

Impact

Around £20 million was reportedly transferred fraudulently. The case highlighted the weakness of relying on video and audio alone as proof of identity.

Lesson Learned

Video and voice should no longer be treated as proof of identity on their own. Any urgent financial instruction must be verified through a second trusted communication channel.

AI PHISHING 2024

Corporate AI Phishing Campaign

Background

Traditional phishing emails often relied on spelling mistakes or poor grammar. However, modern AI tools and large language models can now generate highly polished messages that imitate professional business communication, making them more dangerous.

What Happened?

Attackers used AI tools to create phishing emails that copied the tone, structure, and writing style of legitimate workplace communication. These messages were designed to pressure employees into clicking links or sharing sensitive information. Because the emails looked polished, they were harder to identify.

Why It Was Convincing?

The campaign succeeded because the emails sounded natural, used correct grammar, and included urgency-based language. Instead of obvious mistakes, the messages appeared similar to genuine requests from managers or internal departments.

Impact

AI phishing increases the risk of credential theft, financial fraud, and account compromise. A single convincing message can manipulate multiple employees at once.

Lesson Learned

Users should verify the sender's real email address, avoid acting immediately under pressure, and never trust professional tone alone as proof that an email is safe.

VOICE CLONING 2024

The "Grandparent" Voice Scam

Background

Voice cloning has become one of the most emotionally manipulative forms of AI-enabled fraud. With only a few seconds of audio, attackers can generate a synthetic version of someone's voice and use it to imitate trusted individuals.

What Happened?

Attackers cloned a young person's voice using a short audio sample taken from online content. They then contacted family members, pretending to be in trouble and urgently needing money. The victims believed they were hearing the real voice of someone they loved.

Why It Was Convincing?

The scam worked because the cloned voice sounded familiar and urgent. The attackers used emotional pressure, fear, and time sensitivity to stop the victim from pausing and verifying the situation.

Impact

This type of fraud results in financial loss, emotional distress, and identity-based manipulation. It shows that caller recognition alone is no longer reliable.

Lesson Learned

Voice alone should never be treated as proof of identity. If a call sounds urgent or suspicious, hang up, call back on a known number, and ask a personal verification question.
